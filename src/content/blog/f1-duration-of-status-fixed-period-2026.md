# F-1 Duration of Status Is Ending in 2026: The New Fixed Period of Admission Rule Explained

The **F-1 duration of status** framework that international students have relied on for decades is ending. On **July 17, 2026**, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) published a final rule replacing "duration of status" (D/S) with a **fixed period of admission** for F (academic students), J (exchange visitors), and I (foreign media) nonimmigrants. This guide explains exactly what changes, when, and what F-1 students and OPT participants must do to stay in status.

> **Bottom line:** The final rule takes effect **September 15, 2026**. Until then, F-1 students are still admitted for duration of status. After that date, F-1 students are admitted for a fixed period tied to their Form I-20 program length (capped at four years) plus short arrival and departure windows, and must file an **Extension of Stay (EOS)** with USCIS to go beyond it. This is general information, not legal advice.

## What "duration of status" meant — and what is changing

Under the old system, F-1 students were admitted for **"duration of status" (D/S)** — an open-ended period covering their full course of study plus authorized practical training, with no fixed end date on their Form I-94. As long as you maintained status, you did not need to file for an extension with USCIS.

Under the new **fixed period of admission** rule:

- F-1 students are admitted for a **specific end date**, not an open-ended D/S.
- The fixed period is based on the **program length listed on your Form I-20**, **not to exceed four years**, plus a **30-day arrival window** and an additional **30-day departure window**.
- To stay longer — to finish your program, start a new program, or begin **post-completion OPT or STEM OPT** — you must file an **Extension of Stay (EOS)** with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS).

## Key dates and the transition period

| Date | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Now – Sept 14, 2026 | F-1 students continue to be admitted for **duration of status** |
| **Sept 15, 2026** | Final rule **takes effect**; new admissions use the **fixed period** |
| Aug 31, 2026 | SEVP hosts a live stakeholder webinar (2–3 p.m. EDT) on the changes |

Students already in the U.S. on duration of status will transition under the rule's provisions. Those with valid visas who travel may apply for admission at a port of entry with U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and receive a **new Form I-94 with a fixed period of admission** based on their updated SEVIS record, subject to eligibility and admissibility.

## How the fixed period affects OPT and STEM OPT

This is the change most likely to catch graduates off guard. Under D/S, OPT flowed relatively seamlessly from your study period. Under the new rule, if your admission period is ending and you want to begin **post-completion OPT** or a **STEM OPT extension**, you generally need to file an **Extension of Stay** so your authorized stay covers the training period.

Because OPT and STEM OPT timing now interacts with a hard admission end date, filing early matters. ## What F-1 students should do now

1. **Check your I-20 end date and your I-94.** After Sept 15, 2026, your I-94 will carry a fixed admission end date — know it.
2. **Plan EOS filings early.** If you need more time to finish your program, start a new program, or begin OPT/STEM OPT, budget for USCIS processing time.
3. **Coordinate with your DSO.** Designated school officials can recommend program extensions in SEVIS, but **USCIS adjudicates the EOS** — a DSO recommendation is one factor, not an approval.
4. **Be strategic about travel.** If you travel, CBP may issue a new I-94 with a fixed period based on your SEVIS record and documents.
5. **Watch the effective date.** Admissions before Sept 15, 2026 still use D/S; the fixed period applies on and after that date.

## What this means if you plan to move to H-1B

Many F-1 graduates use STEM OPT as a bridge to an employer-sponsored H-1B. With a fixed admission end date now in play, the cap-gap and change-of-status timing become more sensitive. ## Frequently asked questions

### When does the F-1 duration of status rule actually end?
The DHS final rule was published on July 17, 2026 and **takes effect September 15, 2026**. F-1 students are admitted for duration of status until that date; on and after it, new admissions use a fixed period of admission.

### How long is the new fixed period of admission for F-1 students?
It is based on the program length listed on your Form I-20, **not to exceed four years**, plus a 30-day arrival period and a 30-day departure period. If your program is longer than four years, you would need an Extension of Stay to continue.

### Do I need to file for an extension to do OPT or STEM OPT?
Generally yes. If your fixed admission period would end before or during your training, you must file an **Extension of Stay (EOS)** with USCIS to cover post-completion OPT or STEM OPT. File early — see current [OPT EAD processing times](/uscis-processing-times/opt-ead).

### Does this rule apply to J-1 and other visa categories?
Yes. The same final rule moves F (academic students), J (exchange visitors), and I (foreign media) from duration of status to a fixed period of admission.

### What happens if I am already in the U.S. on duration of status?
You continue on D/S through Sept 14, 2026 and transition under the rule's provisions. If you travel after the effective date, CBP may issue a new Form I-94 with a fixed admission period based on your SEVIS record.
